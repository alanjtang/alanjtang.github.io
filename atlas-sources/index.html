ALAN TANG / ATLAS NOTES

Reading the atlas.

Country profiles were reviewed October 9–10, 2026. The 74 detailed profiles cover the atlas’s highlighted countries and territories, excluding the United States and China. Each card distinguishes relations with Washington and Beijing and includes its own sources. Colors summarize selected security relationships; they do not imply exclusive allegiance or a treaty guarantee.

Borders and occupation

Greenland is a self-governing territory within the Kingdom of Denmark; its NATO context comes through Denmark. Ukraine is shown within its internationally recognized borders, including Crimea. Pink diagonal hatching denotes Russian occupation of Ukrainian territory, not Russian sovereignty.

The occupation overlay uses the Institute for the Study of War and American Enterprise Institute’s Critical Threats Project assessment layer, last edited September 29, 2026. It is simplified for this globe and is not a live frontline or an exact local boundary. Hover over the hatched area for the occupation notice.

Shipping corridors

The ten glowing corridors follow routes computed on Eurostat SeaRoute’s published 20 km maritime network, derived from the ORNL Global Shipping Lane Network (2000), with AIS-based European additions. The network file is dated September 8, 2021.

These are shortest-path models on a historical shipping network—not live AIS tracks, recorded individual voyages, or measurements of 2026 trade volumes. Port locations are approximate and snapped to network nodes. Arctic links north of 60°N are excluded; the Cape route excludes Suez. Glow and line width are decorative. Suez and Hormuz lines do not imply current safe passage or present-day availability.

Corridors span the North Atlantic, trans-Pacific, Asia–Europe through Suez and around the Cape, the Indian Ocean, the Persian Gulf, South America–Asia, Australia–Asia, Panama and Europe–South America.
